\section{Agent District Court and the adj Procedures}

The AgentCourt \code{adj} repository executes disputes through five adjudication procedures~\cite{adj}.  Its programs prepare the case, start model participants, expose permitted actions, store evidence and filings, and record the result.  An optional \code{adjservices} repository supplies managed services and presentation.  The case examined here runs from \code{adj} through its unified \code{adjudicate} command, which selects a procedure and participant configuration from a settings file.

Simple obtains a binary decision and explanation from one model.  Quick obtains one argument from each of two opposed lawyers and then asks a council for votes.  Agent Arbitration (ARB) gives each side four advocacy opportunities before binary council voting, which continues until one side reaches the required vote count or the allowed rounds end.  Agent Arbitration Degree (AARD, selected through the \code{arbd} identifier) gives each side the same four opportunities and asks council members for integer answers from 0 through 100.  Agent District Court (ADC) adds a judge, a clerk, pleadings, discovery, motion practice, trial preparation, bench or jury proceedings, and judgment~\cite{procedures}.  The earlier ARB report examines this market dispute under the arbitration sequence~\cite{arbpaper}.

The lawyers, judge, clerk, and jurors in this case are model agents with assigned procedural authority.  The case record retains their accepted decisions and explanations for examination.

\subsection{Proposition input and civil procedure}

ADC accepts a complaint, a proposition, or a prepared scenario.  Complaint preparation uses model calls to normalize a dispute into a single claim and prepare private party strategies.  Proposition preparation constructs a declaratory case without those intake calls.  The Proponent occupies the plaintiff role and bears the burden of proof, while the Opponent occupies the defendant role.  The programmatic Proposition Tribunal provides jurisdiction for the submitted proposition and limits relief to a declaration, with monetary damages fixed at zero~\cite{adcmanual}.

An ADC opportunity identifies the actor, phase, allowed actions, fixed fields, and whether the actor may pass.  A defendant can receive a preliminary opportunity to raise a supported dismissal ground, followed by an answer.  The parties disclose relevant material, conduct discovery, prepare technical reports, and decide whether to request summary judgment.  Judicial rulings and party choices affect which opportunities follow.  If the case reaches trial, the parties present openings, trial theories, exhibits, rebuttal, proposed instructions, and closings.  A jury votes under the court's instructions, or a judge enters bench findings and conclusions.  The rules also encode post-judgment actions~\cite{practice,arcp}.

The distinction between stored material and trial evidence affects every factual claim.  ADC imports an initial document as a complaint attachment with a case-file identifier.  A party offers a case file as an exhibit through an authorized action during an evidence phase.  Technical reports state methods, results, and limitations of source work.  Pleadings, reports, admitted exhibits, and argument have different functions in the record.  A search result or a private research note can guide an advocate without becoming trial evidence.

\subsection{Model participants and computer tools}

The Go runtime calls the configured model directly for an internal role or exposes the role through a case-owned HTTP API.  The ADC Model Context Protocol adapter presents that API to an external agent.  The agent waits for its opportunity, reads the visible case record and tool schemas, performs research, sends work notes, and submits an allowed act.  The runtime retains authority over acceptance, deadlines, and the next opportunity~\cite{adcmanual,logic}.

Each Pi lawyer has a retained filesystem workspace.  Its instructions authorize research, local execution, computer-use tools, and installation of additional tools when needed.  Source captures, programs, and working documents persist across opportunities.  The two lawyers in this case use GPT-5.6-sol with \code{xhigh} reasoning and Codex subscription authentication.  Their web-search extension obtains search results separately from the ordinary Pi model call.  The court's judge and clerk use the default GPT-5.4 configuration.

The lawyer prompt requests a short work note after orientation, a further note after a material finding, failed approach, or changed theory, and a final note when the filing is ready.  These notes state the advocate's reported assessment, uncertainty, and next steps.  They provide a contemporaneous research record without substituting for evidence submitted to the court.  Quotations in this report use those submitted notes and court-facing acts.  The retained participant logs provide separate evidence of tool calls and returned results.

Jurors receive the case record and the court's instructions.  The local launcher starts Pi juror processes that use ADC tools through MCP.  Their role concerns jury selection and voting from the record.  The lawyers carry the responsibility for developing factual sources and submitting the material on which the jury can rely.

\subsection{Model-pool construction and jury sampling}

A pool record describes a model configuration: model identifier, service endpoint, provider-route constraints, request parameters, and persona.  Provider routes for one named model can differ in availability and supported requests.  The installed pool therefore derives from configuration-level tests and behavior measurements~\cite{poolpaper,poolmanual}.

The generation procedure inventories provider endpoints and checks required tool support.  It screens each candidate through a direct Quick-style \code{submit\_council\_vote} request and a Pi/MCP test following the ARB council sequence.  Accepted configurations answer scored questions.  The deliberation score uses twelve questions from Core20, the project's twenty-question evaluation set.  Those twelve span general knowledge, science and quantitative reasoning, and reasoning.  The score calculates a fraction of correct completed answers per trial, excluding rows with response-metadata errors, and averages over trials containing a counted answer.  Separate measurements cover instruction following and evidence-tool use.  The score describes that question set, without providing an adjudicative-accuracy estimate for this market dispute.

The run that generated the installed pool evaluated 419 configurations.  Its strict deliberation-score threshold above 0.70, together with zero counted provider errors, retained 283.  The next stage collected five responses to each of fourteen behavior prompts, called ``genes'' in the implementation.  These prompts concern subjects such as values, justice, social issues, leadership, and training provenance.  The run used a common generic persona.  Requiring every selected response and embedding request to succeed left 265 configurations~\cite{poolpaper}.

For each behavior prompt, the procedure embedded the responses, retained eight principal components, and selected a K-means cluster count from 2 through 20 by silhouette score.  Each configuration received a representative label for each prompt.  A unique modal label prevailed, while a tie used the sample nearest its assigned cluster center among the tied labels.  The resulting fourteen-label tuple recorded the configuration's observed grouping across the prompts.

Endpoint deduplication grouped rows with the same OpenRouter model identifier, endpoint model identifier, canonical slug, Hugging Face identifier, quantization, and input and output modalities.  Representative selection ranked provider errors, deliberation score, capacity, uptime, latency, price, and stable endpoint identifiers.  The sampling procedure then selected a cluster tuple uniformly and a representative within that tuple uniformly.  The recorded build sampled without replacement and selected at most one row per model identifier.  It produced 100 rows from 148 representatives, covering thirty provider names and 69 cluster tuples~\cite{poolpaper,runbook}.  Three later removals of unavailable configurations left the 97-row pool installed for this case.

Case-time selection operates on that installed pool.  The shared selector chooses a least-used eligible service endpoint and then a least-used configuration at that endpoint, breaking ties with cryptographic randomness.  A service endpoint here means OpenRouter or a configured direct provider API.  Provider routes inside OpenRouter share its endpoint.  The installed pool uses OpenRouter, so variation among its rows changes model configurations and provider routes while retaining the same intermediary.  Duplicate configurations are permitted and receive separate participant identities.  The least-used rule prefers unused configurations before repetition.

ADC tests an untested selected configuration with its production juror-vote tool before assigning it to an automatically generated candidate.  Failed configurations can be replaced during selection.  Voir dire adds the standard questionnaire, counsel's questions, judicial screening, challenges for cause, and peremptory challenges~\cite{juries}.  The approved policy for this case calls for nine jurors and six concurring votes.  ADC's existing failure rules can remove a failed deliberating juror from the eligible body and reduce the effective threshold.  The nominal policy and the eligible jury at verdict can therefore differ.

The preponderance standard governs each juror's assessment of the claim.  The concurrence rule governs how ADC combines those votes into a verdict.  A juror's reported confidence is a further part of its explanation.  The vote count and self-reported confidence have no measured calibration as probabilities that the proposition is true.

\subsection{Lean procedure and replay verification}

ADC, ARB, and AARD use executable Lean rule engines.  ADC's Lean core defines the court state, role-visible views, procedural agenda, authorized decisions, validators, and transitions.  Its Go runtime handles provider requests, external participants, time limits, attempt limits, case-file storage, and output records.  Simple and Quick execute their shorter procedures without a Lean transition engine~\cite{logic,proofs}.

ADC's \code{apply\_decision} operation checks the state version, opportunity identifier, role, decision kind, tool name, and fixed payload fields.  A permitted pass updates the state according to that opportunity's pass rule.  A tool decision returns the authorized court action, which the runtime submits to the engine's \code{step} operation.  Deterministic procedural actions can also pass through \code{step}.  This separation binds a model's accepted act to the opportunity that authorized it.

The maintained ADC proofs establish properties of validation, role authority, state preservation, phase progression, docket effects, decision confinement, replay, and selected terminal outcomes.  Some theorems quantify over states satisfying explicit hypotheses.  Computed theorems check concrete states and certificate examples.  Their scope follows their statements and the encoded procedure.  They establish no general claim about the truth of retrieved evidence or the quality of a model's legal interpretation~\cite{proofs}.

The replay certificate distinguishes deterministic steps from opportunity decisions.  A deterministic transition records its court action.  An opportunity pass records the decision request.  An opportunity tool transition records both that request and the executed action.  Verification repeats the authorization check, requires its resulting action to match the recorded action, executes the transition, and compares the final state with the certificate's claim and the saved case state.  Evidence-byte custody and the historical truth of sources require examination of the accompanying record.

\section{The Market Proposition and Case Setup}

The submitted example asks whether Israel invaded Syria in 2024 under the resolution conditions of a Polymarket market.  The operative condition requires Israel to commence a military offensive intended to establish control over any portion of Syria between September 12 and December 31, 2024, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time.  The market counts the Golan Heights as Israeli territory.  Its permitted sources include official confirmation by Syria, Israel, the United Nations, or a permanent member of the UN Security Council, as well as a consensus of credible reporting~\cite{example}.

The 1974 Disengagement Agreement defines an area of separation between Line A and Line B, with the United Nations Disengagement Observer Force (UNDOF) stationed in that area.  It places Israeli forces west of Line A, with a separate Line A-1 in the Quneitra area, and Syrian forces east of Line B.  It places territory east of Line A under Syrian administration~\cite{disengagement}.  Those line references describe the deployment locations discussed in the case.  The market's Golan Heights exclusion remains a separate term that the parties must apply to the reported locations.

The initial document contains both those rules and the supplied rationale for Polymarket's Yes resolution.  That rationale connects the reported December 8 arrival of Israeli forces at the Syrian summit of Mount Hermon with Benjamin Netanyahu's December 17 statement that forces would remain until an arrangement guaranteed Israel's security.  It also distinguishes the Syrian peak from the Golan Heights.  Both lawyers therefore begin with an account favoring Yes already present.  The proceeding tests the advocates' and court's handling of that supplied account and any subsequently developed evidence.

The ADC run uses fresh participant sessions and receives none of the earlier ARB filings, work notes, or verdict.  Both runs use the same proposition, initial document, lawyer model configuration, enabled search, installed pool, and 25-minute turn timeouts.  The ADC scenario permits up to 500 autopilot turns.  ADC uses its required minimum of six concurring votes, approved by the operator for the nine-member jury.  It retains its default judge, clerk, voir dire, policies, and checked-in prompts~\cite[input settings and generated scenario]{record}.  Procedural differences and fresh model responses prevent attribution of any difference between the runs to ADC alone.

\section{The ADC Proceeding}

The case began at 07:56 UTC on September 19, 2026.  All times for the proceeding are UTC.  The defendant read the market-rules attachment and passed the preliminary dismissal opportunity.  Its work note distinguished the contested merits from a defect in the pleaded claim:
\begin{quote}
Whether the alleged December conduct met 'military offensive' and intent-to-control requirements is a merits/evidence dispute, not a Rule 12 defect.
\end{quote}
The answer accepted the operative market criteria, the Tribunal's authority to decide the proposition, and the restriction to declaratory relief.  It denied that the attachment's recitation of Polymarket's rationale established the facts, required proof of every operative criterion by a preponderance, and preserved disputes over geography, timing, source reliability, offensive character, and intent~\cite[events 1--3 and defense work note at 07:56:29]{record}.

The judge selected jury trial and advanced the case to pretrial proceedings.  At the initial-disclosure opportunity, the plaintiff read the attachment and began searching official Israeli and UN sources and contemporaneous reporting.  Its work note identified the initial document as the only material then in the case record and proposed collecting and importing a focused source packet~\cite[events 4--6 and plaintiff work note at 07:57:10]{record}.

\subsection{Initial disclosures and public-source collection}

The plaintiff imported Syria's December 9 letter to the United Nations as \code{file-0002}, then identified it in its initial disclosures alongside the market attachment.  Both lawyers named public officials and institutional custodians as potential information sources, including Netanyahu, Syrian representatives, Israeli government and military officials, and UN personnel.  Their disclosures distinguished those leads from collected documents, reported no retained expert, and specified zero damages.  The defense had collected no separate documents at that stage~\cite[events 7--9]{record}.

The Syrian letter reports incursions into Jabal al-Shaykh and Qunaytirah together with shelling and demands withdrawal~\cite{syrialetter}.  Before importing it, the plaintiff recorded the remaining inferential question:
\begin{quote}
the letter establishes armed incursion/aggression more directly than the specific intent to establish control
\end{quote}
The note identified the December 17 statement and corroborating reporting as the next research targets~\cite[plaintiff work note at 07:59:37]{record}.

The lawyer searched for official statements, fetched web pages, downloaded PDFs, and inspected publication metadata in retained HTML.  Calls to \code{source\_check} concerning UNDOF observations returned ``unclear.''  The lawyer continued source retrieval and installed Poppler to extract the PDFs with \code{pdftotext}.  Its workspace retained the downloaded documents, extracted text, and written analysis.  The two official PDFs stored by the court match the lawyer's files byte for byte~\cite[plaintiff tool log and registered files]{record}.

At 08:05, the plaintiff imported the Secretary-General's UNDOF report S/2025/154 as \code{file-0003}.  Published on March 12, 2025, the report covers November 19, 2024 through February 18, 2025.  Paragraphs 2 and 6 date Israeli entry and the establishment of checkpoints, roadblocks, and positions to December.  Paragraph 7 records Israel's description of a temporary defensive measure and gives totals for constructed positions and occupied houses as of February 18~\cite{undofreport}.  The source therefore required the lawyer to distinguish dated December observations from later totals.

\subsection{The plaintiff's technical report}

ADC accepted TR-P1, \emph{Source Timeline, Geography, and Indicators of Military Control}, at 08:07.  Its attached analysis, \code{file-0004}, contains a dated source matrix, findings by market criterion, an assessment under the preponderance standard, and limitations.  The filing separates reported conduct, source characterizations, and the lawyer's inferences~\cite[events 11--12 and file-0004]{record}.

The plaintiff relied on the Syrian letter and UNDOF's observations to establish armed entry within the market window.  It used UN distinctions between the Golan, the area of separation, and the Syrian side, together with Associated Press reporting about the Syrian summit, to place at least some conduct beyond the market's Golan exclusion.  It identified the absence of surveyed coordinates and a position-by-position boundary reconstruction.

For offensive character, the report combined tanks, soldiers, an assessed air strike, seizure of positions, movement restrictions, and fortification.  It characterized application of the undefined term ``military offensive'' as an inference.  Its intent analysis treated checkpoints, roadblocks, occupied premises, and fortification as means of controlling territory and connected them to the reported condition for continued deployment:
\begin{quote}
The competing inference is that Israel intended only temporary threat prevention. On balance, that account still describes an intent to control the relevant ground until a condition was met.
\end{quote}
The report concluded that the combined record favored the proposition while preserving Israel's temporary-and-defensive description as contrary evidence~\cite[file-0004, sections 3--4]{record}.

The limitations identified missing operational orders, rules of engagement, eyewitness interviews, satellite measurement, and surveyed coordinates.  They also identified the UN report's retrospective publication, unavailable observation logs, the market's undefined operative term, and the use of an archival reproduction of the prime minister's statement.  The report restricted its use of February persistence to corroboration of control measures begun in December~\cite[event 12 and file-0004, section 5]{record}.

\subsection{The defense's technical report}

The defense imported Israel's December 9 Security Council letter as \code{file-0005}.  The letter acknowledges deployments east of Line A and describes them as limited, temporary measures responding to threats after armed groups attacked UNDOF positions.  It states that Israel's purpose concerned security and disclaims intervention in the conflict between Syrian armed groups~\cite{israelletter}.  The defense compared that contemporaneous statement with the plaintiff's analysis and the full UNDOF report, then filed TR-D1, \emph{Element-by-Element Source and Intent Assessment}, at 08:13~\cite[events 13--18]{record}.

TR-D1 accepted the timing of entry, the presence of troops and tanks, checkpoints, roadblocks, construction, occupied houses, and substantial support for at least some activity beyond the market's Golan exclusion.  Its source matrix separated observations, source characterizations, and the inferences needed to reach the compound proposition.  The report emphasized the attack on UNDOF, the withdrawal of Syrian authorities, and Israel's December 8--9 descriptions of its purpose.  It argued that the UN's observations established conduct without deciding the market's undefined term ``military offensive.''

The defense's central interpretive distinction concerned the purpose of control:
\begin{quote}
A narrower reading gives effect to all words by asking whether establishing control was the operation's objective, rather than an incidental means of threat prevention.
\end{quote}
It treated the December 17 statement as evidence of conditional persistence compatible with the stated security purpose.  It questioned backward inferences from January and February, allowing the possibility of an evolving mission~\cite[file-0006, sections 3E--3F]{record}.  The contrast with TR-P1 was explicit: the plaintiff treated intended territorial control as sufficient even when security supplied the reason for exercising it, while the defense demanded that control constitute an objective distinguishable from threat prevention.

The defense described the Israeli letter as denying the required offensive-control purpose.  The letter's text states a defensive security purpose, temporary deployment, and nonintervention in Syria's internal conflict.  Whether those statements exclude the market's required intent remains an inference.  TR-D1 acknowledged that government letters contain interested characterizations and that internal orders, observation logs, and surveyed coordinates were unavailable.  It also identified a record omission: the AP and prime-ministerial material cited in TR-P1 had yet to be imported as standalone sources~\cite[event 18 and file-0006]{record}.

\subsection{Discovery and the disputed criteria}

The plaintiff responded to the sourcing objection by importing the December 17 Associated Press article as published by PBS NewsHour.  Its work note identified the article's statements about the Syrian summit, continued deployment, fortifications, and anticipated duration.  ADC registered the retained HTML as \code{file-0007} at 08:14~\cite[event 19 and plaintiff work notes at 08:13:51--08:14:34]{record}.

Each side then served five interrogatories.  The plaintiff requested specific admissions or denials of the UNDOF observations, the defense's definition of an offensive, its intent theory and any claimed change of mission, the location of each disputed position, and supporting records and knowledgeable persons.  The defendant asked for the plaintiff's operational definition, evidence of intent at commencement, non-Golan locations, exact official language supporting each criterion, and the source universe and method behind a claimed consensus of reporting~\cite[events 20--21]{record}.  These requests put the parties' interpretations and source-selection methods into discovery.

Before answering, the plaintiff imported a December 8 BBC report as \code{file-0008}.  Its answers identified that report and the AP reporting, distinguished AP republication from independent reporting, and disclaimed an exhaustive media census or a numerical consensus test.  It relied primarily on official sources.  Its answer about official confirmation stated:
\begin{quote}
No single official document repeats the full compound market formula verbatim; Proponent relies on the convergent official confirmations and reasonable inferences from their recorded facts.
\end{quote}
The remaining answers defined offensive action to include a coordinated forward operation using armed personnel and coercive measures to seize, secure, or hold foreign objectives.  They distinguished reported security purposes from inferred control intent, identified missing coordinates and internal orders, and restricted later observations to corroboration~\cite[events 22--24]{record}.

The defense's answers narrowed the factual disagreement.  It accepted the observations as reported, acknowledged the evidentiary force of fortifications and an extended stay, and made no affirmative claim that the identified Qunaytirah, Syrian summit, or east-of-Line-A sites fell within the market-deemed Golan.  It also made no affirmative claim that the mission changed on a particular date.  It defined an offensive as an organized attack or advance directed against an opposing force or objective, while excluding temporary defensive ground-holding based only on the presence of armed personnel.  Its intent answer described the claimed proof failure as distinct from inside knowledge of Israeli decision-makers.  Its objections concerned burden shifting and demands for unknown foreign-government records, with substantive answers following those objections~\cite[event 25]{record}.

\subsection{Document production and context growth}

The plaintiff served ten production requests, and the defense served eleven.  The requests addressed underlying observations, operational records, geography, reporting provenance, source authentication, and market history.  The plaintiff imported an archived reproduction of the December 17 prime-ministerial release as \code{file-0009}.  While preparing its itemized production response, it searched its retained workspace and found another cited but unfiled source: AP's December 9 article.  It imported and produced that capture as \code{file-0010}.  The same inspection identified a purported UNDOF web capture as a 403 error page.  The lawyer recorded that the file supplied no substantive source text~\cite[events 26--28, 44, and 47; plaintiff work note at 08:30:17]{record}.

The plaintiff's itemized response identified eight produced files.  It disclosed that it held no internal orders, UNDOF source logs, surveyed coordinates, complete market or oracle history, or external authority for its operational definitions.  The defense produced seven files and made corresponding disclosures in its response.  Both distinguished available public sources from institutional records outside their possession or control.  ADC accepted their responses at 08:32 and 08:47, respectively~\cite[events 50 and 93]{record}.

Production required repeated decisions about the same documents.  The engine clears its list of passed opportunities after a case update, making previously declined production candidates available again.  The lawyers repeatedly declined copies they regarded as excluded duplicates.  Between the first production and the defense's itemized response, the record contains fifteen production acts and forty-seven passes over twenty-three minutes~\cite[events 29--93]{record}.  The source identifies the mechanism in \code{updateCase}, \code{recordOpportunityPass}, and \code{openOpportunities}~\cite{adccore}.  The repetition increased the number of model calls and produced repetitive work notes even when the lawyers' reasons remained unchanged.

Both lawyers also encountered provider context-window errors during production.  Pi completed its built-in compaction without operator intervention in each session, reporting reductions from approximately 925,000 tokens to estimated contexts of approximately 15,000 tokens.  The lawyers then continued filing, with the court state, registered files, and earlier work notes still available.  Recent plaintiff requests before compaction reported more than 750,000 cached input tokens~\cite[participant process logs and events 41 and 72]{record}.

Work-note acknowledgments also add to the retained context.  The ADC role API returns the current opportunity, including the role-visible case view, after a successful note submission~\cite{roleapi}.  Two plaintiff acknowledgments inspected during admissions each contained approximately 51,000 characters.  Thus even a short note can receive a long response containing repeated case material~\cite[plaintiff process log]{record}.

\subsection{Admissions and evidentiary limits}

The plaintiff served twelve requests for admission, and the defense served fourteen.  Both sets sought source-authenticity admissions.  The plaintiff also isolated dated observations, non-Golan ground-holding, means of controlling access, and the December 17 statements before requesting admission of the compound proposition.  The defense asked about source language and limits already identified during discovery~\cite[events 99 and 105]{record}.

The plaintiff admitted the documents' authenticity and the stated limitations, with qualifications about the inferences they supported.  It acknowledged that no single official document recited the complete market formula, that no produced operational order or contemporaneous statement expressly identified establishing territorial control as the purpose at commencement, and that no produced document connected the assessed aircraft fire operationally to the ground entry.  It admitted the absence of a surveyed position-by-position boundary record and an exhaustive media census.  It continued to rely on the combined official accounts and conduct as proof by inference~\cite[event 111]{record}.

The defense admitted the authenticity of the official documents, the reported December observations, and in-window timing.  Its response to the ninth request stated:
\begin{quote}
Files 0003 and 0005 establish physical ground-holding at least at selected points on Syrian territory outside the Golan Heights deemed Israeli by file-0001.
\end{quote}
It qualified geography beyond the identified locations, admitted that checkpoints and fortifications can control access and use, and acknowledged the December 17 conditional-duration statement and reported fortification instruction.  It denied the final compound request, preserving the distinction between the admitted conduct and its contested offensive character and purpose~\cite[event 118]{record}.  The admissions therefore narrowed the dispute while leaving its decisive classification and intent questions contested.

The plaintiff declined the Rule 37 opportunity to seek an order compelling discovery.  It stated that the defense had answered each request, produced identified controlled sources, and given specific possession-and-control limitations.  Its reason for passing concluded:
\begin{quote}
Merits disagreement should be resolved under Rule 56 or at trial, not through a motion to compel.
\end{quote}
The court recorded the pass at 09:02.  The defense also passed at 09:04, finding no identified responsive record withheld and describing the remaining disputes as questions of weight and inference~\cite[events 124 and 130]{record}.

\subsection{The summary-judgment opportunities}

The plaintiff also declined to seek summary judgment.  Although most historical facts were admitted, it identified offensive character and intended control as disputed material inferences.  It stated that Rule 56 required drawing those inferences for the defendant and that a reasonable jury could accept the defense's distinction between control as an objective and temporary defensive means.  It regarded partial relief on already admitted facts as redundant and selected trial at its 09:06 opportunity~\cite[event 136]{record}.

The defense reached the corresponding conclusion at 09:09.  It described competing reasonable inferences about offensive character and purpose at commencement, acknowledged evidence on both sides, and reserved its burden-of-proof arguments for trial~\cite[event 142]{record}.

The judge entered the pretrial order at 09:11.  It preserved the single declaratory claim and the plaintiff's burden, adopted the recorded admissions as established for trial subject to their qualifications, and identified the questions left for decision.  Those questions included offensive classification, intent at commencement, the weight of the Israeli and Syrian characterizations, the significance of the December 17 evidence, and the sufficiency of the combined official sources.  It listed all ten registered files as potential exhibits subject to objections and later rulings.  The order confined trial to those issues~\cite[event 148]{record}.
