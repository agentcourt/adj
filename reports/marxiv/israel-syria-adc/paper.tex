\section{Agent District Court and the adj Procedures}

The AgentCourt \code{adj} repository executes disputes through five adjudication procedures~\cite{adj}.  Its programs prepare the case, start model participants, expose permitted actions, store evidence and filings, and record the result.  An optional \code{adjservices} repository supplies managed services and presentation.  The case examined here runs from \code{adj} through its unified \code{adjudicate} command, which selects a procedure and participant configuration from a settings file.

Simple obtains a binary decision and explanation from one model.  Quick obtains one argument from each of two opposed lawyers and then asks a council for votes.  Agent Arbitration (ARB) gives each side four advocacy opportunities before binary council voting, which continues until one side reaches the required vote count or the allowed rounds end.  Agent Arbitration Degree (AARD, selected through the \code{arbd} identifier) gives each side the same four opportunities and asks council members for integer answers from 0 through 100.  Agent District Court (ADC) adds a judge, a clerk, pleadings, discovery, motion practice, trial preparation, bench or jury proceedings, and judgment~\cite{procedures}.  The earlier ARB report examines this market dispute under the arbitration sequence~\cite{arbpaper}.

``Lawyer,'' ``judge,'' ``clerk,'' and ``juror'' name assigned roles in the software.  The participants in this case are model agents.  Their decisions concern a submitted proposition under a specified evidence standard.  The role names describe their procedural authority, and the stored acts permit examination of what they did with that authority.

\subsection{Proposition input and civil procedure}

ADC accepts a complaint, a proposition, or a prepared scenario.  Complaint preparation uses model calls to normalize a dispute into a single claim and prepare private party strategies.  Proposition preparation constructs a declaratory case without those intake calls.  The Proponent occupies the plaintiff role and bears the burden of proof, while the Opponent occupies the defendant role.  The programmatic Proposition Tribunal provides jurisdiction for the submitted proposition and limits relief to a declaration, with monetary damages fixed at zero~\cite{adcmanual}.

An ADC opportunity identifies the actor, phase, allowed actions, fixed fields, and whether the actor may pass.  A defendant can receive a preliminary opportunity to raise a supported dismissal ground, followed by an answer.  The parties disclose relevant material, conduct discovery, prepare technical reports, and decide whether to request summary judgment.  Judicial rulings and party choices affect which opportunities follow.  If the case reaches trial, the parties present openings, trial theories, exhibits, rebuttal, proposed instructions, and closings.  A jury votes under the court's instructions, or a judge enters bench findings and conclusions.  The rules also encode post-judgment actions~\cite{practice,arcp}.

The distinction between stored material and trial evidence affects every factual claim.  ADC imports an initial document as a complaint attachment with a case-file identifier.  A party offers a case file as an exhibit through an authorized action during an evidence phase.  Technical reports state methods, results, and limitations of source work.  Pleadings, reports, admitted exhibits, and argument have different functions in the record.  A search result or a private research note can guide an advocate without becoming trial evidence.

\subsection{Model participants and computer tools}

The Go runtime calls the configured model directly for an internal role or exposes the role through a case-owned HTTP API.  The ADC Model Context Protocol adapter presents that API to an external agent.  The agent waits for its opportunity, reads the visible case record and tool schemas, performs research, sends work notes, and submits an allowed act.  The runtime retains authority over acceptance, deadlines, and the next opportunity~\cite{adcmanual,logic}.

Each Pi lawyer has a retained filesystem workspace.  Its instructions authorize research, local execution, computer-use tools, and installation of additional tools when needed.  Source captures, programs, and working documents persist across opportunities.  The two lawyers in this case use GPT-5.6-sol with \code{xhigh} reasoning and Codex subscription authentication.  Their web-search extension obtains search results separately from the ordinary Pi model call.  The court's judge and clerk use the default GPT-5.4 configuration.

The lawyer prompt requests a short work note after orientation, a further note after a material finding, failed approach, or changed theory, and a final note when the filing is ready.  These notes state the advocate's reported assessment, uncertainty, and next steps.  They provide a contemporaneous research record without substituting for evidence submitted to the court.  Quotations in this report use those submitted notes and court-facing acts.  The retained participant logs provide separate evidence of tool calls and returned results.

Jurors receive the case record and the court's instructions.  The local launcher starts Pi juror processes that use ADC tools through MCP.  Their role concerns jury selection and voting from the record.  The lawyers carry the responsibility for developing factual sources and submitting the material on which the jury can rely.

\subsection{Model-pool construction and jury sampling}

A pool record describes a model configuration: model identifier, service endpoint, provider-route constraints, request parameters, and persona.  Provider routes for one named model can differ in availability and supported requests.  The installed pool therefore derives from configuration-level tests and behavior measurements~\cite{poolpaper,poolmanual}.

The generation procedure inventories provider endpoints and checks required tool support.  It screens each candidate through a direct Quick-style \code{submit\_council\_vote} request and a Pi/MCP test following the ARB council sequence.  Accepted configurations answer scored questions.  The deliberation score uses twelve questions from Core20, the project's twenty-question evaluation set.  Those twelve span general knowledge, science and quantitative reasoning, and reasoning.  The score calculates a fraction of correct completed answers per trial, excluding rows with response-metadata errors, and averages over trials containing a counted answer.  Separate measurements cover instruction following and evidence-tool use.  The score describes that question set, without providing an adjudicative-accuracy estimate for this market dispute.

The run that generated the installed pool evaluated 419 configurations.  Its strict deliberation-score threshold above 0.70, together with zero counted provider errors, retained 283.  The next stage collected five responses to each of fourteen behavior prompts, called ``genes'' in the implementation.  These prompts concern subjects such as values, justice, social issues, leadership, and training provenance.  The run used a common generic persona.  Requiring every selected response and embedding request to succeed left 265 configurations~\cite{poolpaper}.

For each behavior prompt, the procedure embedded the responses, retained eight principal components, and selected a K-means cluster count from 2 through 20 by silhouette score.  Each configuration received a representative label for each prompt.  A unique modal label prevailed, while a tie used the sample nearest its assigned cluster center among the tied labels.  The resulting fourteen-label tuple recorded the configuration's observed grouping across the prompts.

Endpoint deduplication grouped rows with the same OpenRouter model identifier, endpoint model identifier, canonical slug, Hugging Face identifier, quantization, and input and output modalities.  Representative selection ranked provider errors, deliberation score, capacity, uptime, latency, price, and stable endpoint identifiers.  The sampling procedure then selected a cluster tuple uniformly and a representative within that tuple uniformly.  The recorded build sampled without replacement and selected at most one row per model identifier.  It produced 100 rows from 148 representatives, covering thirty provider names and 69 cluster tuples~\cite{poolpaper,runbook}.  Three later removals of unavailable configurations left the 97-row pool installed for this case.

Case-time selection operates on that installed pool.  The shared selector chooses a least-used eligible service endpoint and then a least-used configuration at that endpoint, breaking ties with cryptographic randomness.  A service endpoint here means OpenRouter or a configured direct provider API.  Provider routes inside OpenRouter share its endpoint.  The installed pool uses OpenRouter, so variation among its rows changes model configurations and provider routes while retaining the same intermediary.  Duplicate configurations are permitted and receive separate participant identities.  The least-used rule prefers unused configurations before repetition.

ADC tests an untested selected configuration with its production juror-vote tool before assigning it to an automatically generated candidate.  Failed configurations can be replaced during selection.  Voir dire adds the standard questionnaire, counsel's questions, judicial screening, challenges for cause, and peremptory challenges~\cite{juries}.  The approved policy for this case calls for nine jurors and six concurring votes.  ADC's existing failure rules can remove a failed deliberating juror from the eligible body and reduce the effective threshold.  The final record must therefore establish both the nominal policy and the jurors who remained eligible when the court derived its result.

The preponderance standard governs each juror's assessment of the claim.  The concurrence rule governs how ADC combines those votes into a verdict.  A juror's reported confidence is a further part of its explanation.  The vote count and self-reported confidence have no measured calibration as probabilities that the proposition is true.

\subsection{Lean procedure and replay verification}

ADC, ARB, and AARD use executable Lean rule engines.  ADC's Lean core defines the court state, role-visible views, procedural agenda, authorized decisions, validators, and transitions.  Its Go runtime handles provider requests, external participants, time limits, attempt limits, case-file storage, and output records.  Simple and Quick execute their shorter procedures without a Lean transition engine~\cite{logic,proofs}.

ADC's \code{apply\_decision} operation checks the state version, opportunity identifier, role, decision kind, tool name, and fixed payload fields.  A permitted pass updates the state according to that opportunity's pass rule.  A tool decision returns the authorized court action, which the runtime submits to the engine's \code{step} operation.  Deterministic procedural actions can also pass through \code{step}.  This separation binds a model's accepted act to the opportunity that authorized it.

The maintained ADC proofs establish properties of validation, role authority, state preservation, phase progression, docket effects, decision confinement, replay, and selected terminal outcomes.  Some theorems quantify over states satisfying explicit hypotheses.  Computed theorems check concrete states and certificate examples.  Their scope follows their statements and the encoded procedure.  They establish no general claim about the truth of retrieved evidence or the quality of a model's legal interpretation~\cite{proofs}.

The replay certificate distinguishes deterministic steps from opportunity decisions.  A deterministic transition records its court action.  An opportunity pass records the decision request.  An opportunity tool transition records both that request and the executed action.  Verification repeats the authorization check, requires its resulting action to match the recorded action, executes the transition, and compares the final state with the certificate's claim and the saved case state.  Evidence-byte custody and the historical truth of sources require examination of the accompanying record.

\section{The Market Proposition and Case Setup}

The submitted example asks whether Israel invaded Syria in 2024 under the resolution conditions of a Polymarket market.  The operative condition requires Israel to commence a military offensive intended to establish control over any portion of Syria between September 12 and December 31, 2024, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time.  The market counts the Golan Heights as Israeli territory.  Its permitted sources include official confirmation by Syria, Israel, the United Nations, or a permanent member of the UN Security Council, as well as a consensus of credible reporting~\cite{example}.

The 1974 Disengagement Agreement defines an area of separation between Line A and Line B, with the United Nations Disengagement Observer Force (UNDOF) stationed in that area.  It places Israeli forces west of Line A, with a separate Line A-1 in the Quneitra area, and Syrian forces east of Line B.  It places territory east of Line A under Syrian administration~\cite{disengagement}.  Those line references describe the deployment locations discussed in the case.  The market's Golan Heights exclusion remains a separate term that the parties must apply to the reported locations.

The initial document contains both those rules and the supplied rationale for Polymarket's Yes resolution.  That rationale connects the reported December 8 arrival of Israeli forces at the Syrian summit of Mount Hermon with Benjamin Netanyahu's December 17 statement that forces would remain until an arrangement guaranteed Israel's security.  It also distinguishes the Syrian peak from the Golan Heights.  Both lawyers therefore begin with an account favoring Yes already present.  The proceeding tests the advocates' and court's handling of that supplied account and any subsequently developed evidence.

The ADC run uses fresh participant sessions and receives none of the earlier ARB filings, work notes, or verdict.  Both runs use the same proposition, initial document, lawyer model configuration, enabled search, installed pool, and 25-minute turn timeouts.  The ADC scenario permits up to 500 autopilot turns.  ADC uses its required minimum of six concurring votes, approved by the operator for the nine-member jury.  It retains its default judge, clerk, voir dire, policies, and checked-in prompts.  Procedural differences and fresh model responses prevent attribution of any difference between the runs to ADC alone.

\section{The ADC Proceeding}

The case began at 04:28 UTC on September 19, 2026.  All times for the proceeding are UTC.  The defendant read the initial attachment and declined a preliminary dismissal motion.  Its work note distinguished a dispute about the evidence from a defect in the pleading:
\begin{quote}
Whether those acts amount to an offensive intended to establish control is a factual merits dispute.
\end{quote}
The defendant then filed an answer accepting the Tribunal's jurisdiction, the existence of the dispute, declaratory-only relief, and the operative market rules.  It admitted that the attachment recited Polymarket's rationale but denied that the recited assertions established the proposition.  The defense required proof of offensive character, contemporaneous intent, qualifying territory, and conduct within the specified dates~\cite[events 1--3 and work note at 04:29]{record}.

Both parties served initial disclosures after the judge selected jury trial and advanced the case to pretrial proceedings.  Each identified Netanyahu as a named person with relevant public-source knowledge and government, military, and UN custodians as possible sources.  They distinguished those research leads from collected evidence.  The plaintiff disclosed:
\begin{quote}
At present, file-0001 is the only such material in plaintiff's possession, custody, or control; additional identified sources will be produced and these disclosures supplemented.
\end{quote}
The defendant likewise stated that it possessed no additional collected responsive material.  Both claimed zero monetary damages.  The disclosures established the starting documentary record and the parties' stated plans for developing it~\cite[events 7--8]{record}.

\subsection{Source retrieval and the first imported document}

The plaintiff's technical-report investigation began with searches for Israeli and UN statements and reporting by Reuters, Associated Press, and the BBC.  Its work plan separated timing, the character of the military operation, qualifying geography, and intent.  It also distinguished reported facts from the ultimate application of the market definition~\cite[work note at 04:30:59 and plaintiff tool log]{record}.

Source collection required several methods.  The search extension returned page summaries and readable text, while some official pages returned incomplete extraction or JavaScript-rendering errors.  A local download script initially failed because the Python \code{requests} package was unavailable.  The lawyer replaced it with standard-library HTTP calls, retained downloaded files in its workspace, and encountered HTTP 403 responses from further sources.  Its separate \code{source\_check} request about UNDOF's reported positions returned ``unclear,'' with some irrelevant dated pages among its results.  That tool response provided no resolved verification of the claim.

The plaintiff obtained a Syrian letter to the United Nations, installed \code{poppler-utils} in its container, and extracted the PDF's text with \code{pdftotext}.  After reading it, the lawyer corrected its filename from S/2024/887 to A/79/687--S/2024/888.  The letter, dated December 9 and distributed December 11, 2024, attributed incursions in Jabal al-Shaykh and Qunaytirah to Israeli forces, alleged contemporaneous shelling, and demanded withdrawal from recently invaded areas.  The plaintiff imported the PDF as \code{file-0002} at 04:43.  The document supplied a contemporaneous Syrian governmental account from a source category named in the market rules.  Its allegations and the weight they deserved remained questions for the parties and court~\cite[event 9 and file-0002]{record}.

The plaintiff's work note at 04:42:55 described the letter as stronger official confirmation than it had anticipated and identified corroborating UNDOF and press accounts.  That note followed its research-plan note by eleven minutes and fifty-six seconds.  The tool log records research throughout the interval, but the submitted notes provide no intermediate account of the findings or failed approaches during it.

\subsection{The plaintiff's technical report}

The court accepted TR-P1 at 04:44.  The filing linked the Syrian PDF and presented a criterion-by-criterion analysis of timing, offensive character, geography, control intent, and the permitted sources.  A longer chronology remained in the plaintiff's workspace at this stage.  The filed report relied on the Syrian letter's account of army incursions and shelling, UNDOF's observations of movement and continuing presence, and press accounts of taking positions in the Syrian buffer zone and on Mount Hermon~\cite[event 10]{record}.

For intent, the plaintiff connected the reported seizure of positions with Netanyahu's statements that deployment would continue until a satisfactory security arrangement.  It argued that the security condition described the intended duration of possession:
\begin{quote}
Holding strategic terrain for an indefinite, condition-based period until a future arrangement is control. The rule requires intended control, not annexation or permanence.
\end{quote}
For offensive character, it characterized the cross-boundary advance to seize and hold strategic ground as an offensive despite Israel's defensive rationale.  Those propositions supplied the interpretive steps between the described conduct and the market's conjunctive rule.

The filing disclosed limits on both its sources and its methods.  It described Syria as an interested official source and acknowledged that UNDOF's observations did not apply the market term ``invasion.''  It identified the failure to retrieve the original Israeli prime minister's page and its use of a contemporaneous attributed reproduction and Reuters.  It also acknowledged Israel's limited, temporary, and defensive descriptions, the absence of a coordinate-level boundary test or raw satellite analysis, and the lack of a separate definition of ``military offensive.''  The report thus stated its conclusion while identifying the factual and interpretive questions the defense could contest~\cite[event 10, limitations]{record}.

\subsection{The defense's technical report}

The defense retrieved Israel's letter of December 9, S/2024/887, extracted its text locally, and imported the PDF as \code{file-0003} at 04:48.  The letter acknowledged deployment east of Line A and described attacks on UNDOF posts by armed groups as the precipitating event.  It characterized Israel's measures as limited, temporary, and defensive, stated a sole focus on security, and reaffirmed commitment to the disengagement framework.  The defense also downloaded and read the Syrian letter.  Its work note said the Israeli account supported its purpose argument while leaving physical presence and the inference of control intact~\cite[event 12, file-0003, and work note at 04:48:04]{record}.

TR-D1, accepted at 04:50, compared the two letters dated the same day.  It conceded timely deployment east of Line A in qualifying Syrian territory and evidence of tactical holding.  The dispute concerned the interpretation of those facts and the sources' competing accounts of purpose.  The report explained its treatment of the market's authority rule:
\begin{quote}
The authority rule identifies acceptable source classes; it does not make one party's characterization dispositive of the proposition's more specific functional definition.
\end{quote}
That distinction accepted Syria as a permitted source while contesting the plaintiff's reliance on its characterization of the events~\cite[event 13]{record}.

The defense identified two inferential disputes.  First, the Syrian letter placed army incursions and shelling together without describing an operational link between strikes and a territorial-control campaign.  Second, the defense distinguished tactical control used for force protection from control as the intended object of the operation.  It read the December 17 statement as evidence of expected persistence, while questioning what the later statement established about intent at commencement.  The report acknowledged that the same statement reasonably supported the plaintiff and that the defense could not exclude a later change in purpose.  Both national letters, it stated, were interested accounts~\cite[event 13, summary and limitations]{record}.

The defense's substantive notes followed its initial research note by two minutes thirty-seven seconds and then two minutes eight seconds.  They recorded the discovery of the Israeli letter, its adverse as well as favorable implications, and the intended scope of the report.  The private source files and extracted text remained available for later turns.

\subsection{Interrogatories and the narrowing of the dispute}

Each party served five interrogatories.  The plaintiff asked the defense to identify deployment points, military acts, purpose evidence, withdrawal conditions, possible mission changes, and the factual basis for its remaining denial.  The defense asked the plaintiff to identify the alleged offensive, evidence of intent at commencement, qualifying territory, each official confirmation, and the independent basis of its claimed reporting consensus.  Both sets required the respondent to separate public-source facts from additional inferences~\cite[events 15--16]{record}.

The plaintiff's answers narrowed its reliance on the relation between ground operations and strikes.  It acknowledged that the Syrian letter established common timing and context without supplying particular unit names, strike targets, or coordinates.  It then stated that ``proof that every strike formed one operation is not essential'' because the ground entry, seizure, and holding independently satisfied its interpretation of the rule.  The answer also distinguished the official accounts' reported acts from counsel's application of ``offensive'' and ``control intent''~\cite[event 17, answers 1 and 4]{record}.

The plaintiff narrowed its claimed consensus as well:
\begin{quote}
The consensus claimed concerns core facts---cross-boundary deployment, seizure/holding, Syrian location, and condition-based duration---not unanimity over the label `invasion' or Israel's motive.
\end{quote}
The response described each reporting source's reliance on official statements and other reporting, acknowledging that some observations came from those sources rather than firsthand reporting.  That qualification limited the earlier report's broader appeal to independent corroboration~\cite[event 17, answer 5]{record}.

The defense's answers accepted crossing east of Line A, occupation and holding of positions including the Syrian Mount Hermon summit, continued presence within the market period, and Syrian and UN objections.  It acknowledged that the known withdrawal condition depended on a security arrangement rather than a fixed date.  It also stated that it possessed no operational orders or internal decision records and could not determine whether the mission's purpose had changed.  Limited objections distinguished the litigant from Israel or the IDF, whose nonpublic records it did not control.  The defense retained its contention that the agreed conduct supported a temporary-security explanation as well as the plaintiff's offensive-and-control interpretation~\cite[event 18]{record}.

\subsection{Document production and retained source copies}

The plaintiff served eight requests for production, and the defense served seven.  The defense requested the retained sources behind TR-P1, including complete articles, official statements, geographic material, evidence of purpose and duration, contrary sources, and collection metadata.  Those requests required the plaintiff to distinguish its research conclusions from the documents it could supply~\cite[events 19--20]{record}.

At 05:00, the plaintiff imported and produced \code{file-0004}, an archive containing five retained HTML pages and readable extracts: Associated Press as republished by KSAT, BBC, Reuters as republished by The Straits Times, the December 17 statement attributed to Israel's prime minister as reproduced by GlobalSecurity, and UNDOF's December 13 statement.  The archive included a manifest with source and retrieval information.  The lawyer assembled it from files retained during its earlier research, excluding private notes and legal analysis~\cite[events 24--25, file-0004, and plaintiff tool log]{record}.

The response identified the substitutes and omissions.  The plaintiff had obtained republications where original pages blocked retrieval, had no original \code{gov.il} capture or separate recording, and had not downloaded remotely embedded media.  It possessed no separate GIS analysis, satellite imagery, operational order, internal assessment, or mission-change record.  It identified the Israeli letter and the archive's temporary-and-defensive language as responsive contrary evidence.  The response made the limits of counsel's source collection available for examination by the defense~\cite[event 26]{record}.

The proceeding remains in progress.  The remaining production, admissions, motion practice, trial, and judgment will be described from the completed record.
